The treasury challenge

Knowing what is in the bank is not the same as knowing where the business stands.

Recognise the pattern

Treasury is working. But is it working as one?

Treasury complexity often grows gradually: more accounts, more entities, more systems and more manual work. Eventually, the effort required to understand the position becomes a constraint on the business.

01

Cash is spread across the organisation.

What you may be seeing

Cash balances sit across multiple banks, accounts and entities. Consolidating the position takes time, and leadership cannot always see available cash when it matters.

02

Forecasts struggle to keep pace.

What you may be seeing

Cash flow forecasts rely on spreadsheets, manual updates and assumptions that are difficult to validate. The forecast quickly becomes outdated as the business changes.

03

Liquidity is managed reactively.

What you may be seeing

Surplus cash, funding needs and upcoming obligations are not considered together. Decisions happen late, leaving opportunities or risks unidentified.

04

Banking operations are fragmented.

What you may be seeing

Bank relationships, payments, reporting and reconciliation involve separate processes. Teams spend too much time coordinating information and resolving operational gaps.

The shift

From scattered information to one treasury view.

The same banks, systems and people, connected around a single picture of cash, so the position is understood rather than assembled.

Today

Information lives in silos. Each update is gathered by hand, and the picture is out of date by the time it is complete.

→
Connected

Data flows into a shared view. Leadership sees available cash, upcoming needs and risks together, when it matters.

Where clarity matters

Bring the moving parts of Treasury together.

A more effective Treasury function connects cash visibility, forecasting, liquidity, working capital and banking operations. Each area needs to work as part of a wider financial picture, not as an isolated activity.
